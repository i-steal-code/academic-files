# H2 Physics (9478) Paper 3, November 2026: outlook and findings

Version 2026-11-13, made after Paper 2. Analysis and findings only: no question lists. All numbers are also in `paper3-analysis.json` and the CSV files.

## Method

- Both papers as one pool: commonness is judged over Papers 2 and 3 together; Paper-3-only reasoning ("rare in Paper 3") is not used.
- Topic chances are conditioned on the topic's 2026 Paper 2 status (tested or not), from A-level 2016-2025 (10 pairs) and the 16 school pairs of 2026, blended 0.6 A-level / 0.4 schools.
- "In Paper 3 at all" = 4 or more marks anywhere in Paper 3. "Whole Section A question" = the topic leads (has the most marks in) a Section A question of 5 or more marks; the headline band runs from a stricter count by an independent checker (the topic itself has 5 or more marks) up to this count. "Leads a Section B option" = the topic leads an option and has 8 or more of its marks.
- Expected Section A marks (of 55): mean Section A marks over sittings with the same Paper 2 status, blended, scaled to 55; mechanics conditioned on Paper 2 mechanics of 21 or more marks.
- Within-topic question-type weights: all 188 papers (A-level 2016-2025 Papers 2 and 3; school prelims 2022-2025, 9749 syllabus; school prelims 2026, 9478), every part including data-based questions, parts outside 9478 dropped, a part's marks split equally over its types; each source's share inside the topic blended 0.5 A-level / 0.25 prelims 2022-2025 / 0.25 prelims 2026. New 9478 content (no marks possible in old-syllabus papers) takes its share from the 2026 papers.
- The 2026 Paper 3 view of a topic removes the question types the 2026 Paper 2 tested and renormalises the rest.
- Checks: an independent rebuild with sensitivity runs, a critic, a syllabus check and a review of the final figures.

Sources: 20 A-level papers (2016-2025, Papers 2 and 3), 136 school prelim papers 2022-2025 (9749 syllabus) and 32 school prelim papers 2026 (9478), 188 in all. Paired sittings for the topic chances: 10 A-level (2016-2025) and 16 schools (2026). MI 2026 (a 9749-format paper) is left out. Specimen papers have no mark scheme and are used by topic label only.

## Mechanics

- 2026 Paper 2 mechanics was about 31 marks (circular motion 11, the data-based question about 20); the most in any A-level Paper 2 from 2016 to 2025 was 23.
- Sittings with 21 or more mechanics marks in Paper 2: Paper 3 Section A had a general mechanics question in Cambridge 1 of 3 and schools 5 of 7. With a cut-off of 18: 4 of 7 and 8 of 10.
- So about an even chance of one 8-11 mark mechanics question, on a chapter Paper 2 skipped. In those sittings the untested chapters led as follows: forces and moments 6 times, collisions 3, projectiles 2.
- Expected about 7 marks of mechanics in Section A (5-9), against about 12 when Paper 2 has less mechanics.
- About a 15-20% chance that a Section B option is mechanics (collisions 13%, projectiles 7%). Cambridge did this in 2020 after a mechanics-heavy Paper 2: a 20-mark collisions option.
- Projectile motion: about a 1-in-5 chance of a whole question, usually mixing a launch calculation and air resistance; the two whole Cambridge Paper 3 projectile questions (2017, 2019) were mainly air resistance.
- Circular motion has no separate question after this Paper 2, but it is used inside orbits and r = mv/Bq.

## Structure and patterns

- Paper 3 (9478): 2 hours, 75 marks. Section A: 55 marks of structured questions, all compulsory (the specimen has 7 questions of 5-11 marks). Section B: 20 marks, one of two questions.
- Section A in the 26 Paper 3s analysed (A-level 2016-2025 and 16 schools 2026): 5-8 questions, mostly 7, of 3-14 marks. When a topic comes up in Section A it is usually one question of about 8-10 marks.
- Papers 2 and 3 are one pool: a topic can sit in either paper. A one-question topic (nuclear, thermal, EM forces, electric fields, gravitation, oscillations, EM induction) with no question of its own in Paper 2 was in Paper 3 44 times out of 45 (A-level 2017-2025); one with its own Paper 2 question, 8 of 18.
- Repeats after Paper 2 (whole question in Paper 2 and again in Paper 3 Section A): single-idea topics almost never come back (oscillations 0/8, EM induction 0/8, circular motion 0/4, thermal 1/10, forces 1/15); many-part topics about 1 time in 3 (quantum 6/16, superposition 5/15, nuclear 5/13, gravitation 5/11, circuits 5/14).
- When a Paper 2 topic came back in Paper 3, only about 12% of its Paper 3 marks repeated a Paper 2 question type (62 cases). Parts Paper 2 tested are low, not zero: Paper 3 keeps about 55-70% of their normal rate.
- Schools 2026 repeat 23% of their Paper 2 topics in Paper 3; Cambridge (A-level 2016-2025) 36%.
- Whole question (lead, 5+ marks) in either paper, 26 sittings: oscillations 26/26, circuits 26/26, superposition 25/26, nuclear 25/26, gravitation 24/26, quantum 24/26, EM induction 24/26, forces 22/26, electric fields 19/26, EM forces 19/26, currents 16/26, projectiles 14/26, collisions 14/26.
- Merged questions let one question cover two untested chapters: velocity selector (electric fields + EM forces), capacitor in a circuit (electric fields + circuits), orbit (gravitation + circular motion), a.c. (currents + EM induction), nuclear recoil (nuclear + collisions).
- First 9478 sitting: Paper 2 showcased the particle in a box; capacitance and RC are the main new content still untested. In the 2026 school papers, capacitors and RC are about 40% of the d.c. circuits marks and capacitance about 22% of the electric fields marks (see the ★ types). The 9749 years cannot contain them, so shares pooled with those years understate them; here their share is taken from the 2026 papers.
- 9478 Section A is 55 marks (9749: 60), so the untested topics compete for one fewer slot.
- 9478 keeps only the threshold-frequency argument of the photoelectric effect.
- A-level 2016 is the 9646 format (Section A 40 marks; Section B two of three): weight it down or leave it out.
- Electricity and magnetism together: about 20 of the 55 expected Section A marks (19-21.5 in every check).

## What is robust and what is fragile

Robust:

- Electricity and magnetism about 20 of 55 Section A marks (19-21.5 in every check)
- Gravitation, oscillations and nuclear about 5-6 expected Section A marks each
- Superposition about 3, quantum about 2.5, measurement 1.4
- The order: electricity first; then oscillations, gravitation, nuclear; the topics Paper 2 used heavily drop

Fragile:

- The mechanics total (5.4-8.9, depends on the source weight and the heavy-Paper-2 cut-off) and the split inside it (forces 0.8-3.6)
- Thermal (1.8-3.1; range 0-4): Cambridge 2 of 3 sittings with the same status against schools 0 of 8
- Currents: 5.3 rests on 2 Cambridge sittings (2 of 2) against schools 5 of 11; at a lead threshold of 5 it is 3.4
- Section B percentages: each rests on 5-13 sittings and on the definition of "leads an option"
- Exact Section B pair: 1 in 3 from 8 sittings; forecasters gave 20-40%

## Effort rule

1. Floor first: every topic with about a 1-in-3 or higher chance of a whole question gets to "can do a standard question", in order of importance, before any topic gets deep work.
2. Cheap insurance: one pass each of collisions, projectile motion, the untested quantum parts, thermal leftovers, wave motion and measurement (uncertainty).
3. Section B depth: nuclear and oscillations to full 20-mark depth, plus one electricity area (charged particles in E and B fields, or capacitors and RC). That set covers at least one option about 2 times in 3, and more after this Paper 2.
4. Then the remaining time by expected marks.
5. Decide effort by the chance of a whole question (questions are lumpy, about 8 marks) as well as expected marks; never argue "rare in Paper 3" for a topic that may simply have sat in Paper 2.
