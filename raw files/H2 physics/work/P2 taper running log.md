### Tier 1: drill

**Habits (every question)**
- Explanations written like working: principle or force, then the step it causes, then the result asked for. (Marker's intent, 13 leaks: the largest source of lost marks)
- Draw the set-up before any geometry or vector equation, even when it feels obvious. (Ch 14: about 8 marks)
- Stuck for more than about 2 minutes: write the defining relation for the quantity asked for and work forward. If that doesn't move, mark the part, go on, come back. (Ch 19: three stalls, about 19 min over)
- Unfamiliar or unintuitive part (user, 11 Nov): go to first principles. Ask what changes when this happens (speed, flux, which side is in the field, which energy store) and what stays the same, then follow the change to the quantity asked for. It finds the marker's point when the model is unseen. It would have caught Ch 18 (c)(iii) (fully inside: nothing changes, so ε = 0) and (d)(ii) (the magnet speeds up, so NΦ changes faster). It is the opposite of adjusting numbers until a check fits.
- **A symbol the question defines is used exactly as defined** (Epoch 2 TMJC Q8 (b)(ix), 2 marks): "σ is the absolute uncertainty of the radius" means one radius, 0.001 m. Don't re-derive it as the uncertainty of Δr; the 3σ in the criterion is the safety margin.
- **"Suggest an explanation for these observations": one point per observation, including any the stem hands you as data** (Epoch 2 JPJC Q9 (d)(iv)).
- **2 marks on a one-line "explain" means two points:** the principle in words (W = Fs cos θ, with θ = 90°), then the conclusion (no work, constant speed) (Epoch 2 TMJC Q8 (b)(v)).
- **Slow start (Epoch 3 P1 and the A-level P2):** the first two questions ate the paper's leeway. While waiting to start, recite the relations for the likeliest topics (φ = −GM/r, g = GM/r², E = −dV/dx, F = qE, r = mv/Bq, τ = RC, ½CV², BE bookkeeping) so the first question isn't the warm-up. Apply the 45-second skip test from question 1, not from question 3.
- **Read the stem's end state** (Epoch 3 P3 EJC Q3 (b)): "the ice remains ice" means mcΔθ only, no latent heat. Underline the final state as you underline time-words.
- **Check a formula's units before using it** (Epoch 3 P2 HCI Q3 (c)): ½mv is momentum-like, not energy; ½mv² gives J.
- **"Change in X" is signed: ΔX = final − initial.** A drop gives a negative answer, and markers deduct a mark for a positive one. (Epoch 3 HCI Q2 (b)(ii))
- **Circuit changes: write V = IR for each component before stating any direction.** R up → total R up → I down (same I everywhere in series) → p.d. across the fixed resistors down → p.d. across the changed component up, since the e.m.f. is shared. Every resistor in the loop goes into the total. (Epoch 3 DHS Q1 (b), (d))
- Underline every command word. "Determine" means a value with unit and direction.
- Sanity-check values against known ones: magnitude, units, sign. (Calculator entry, 5 leaks)
- In a paper, open each new question by naming its topic and the two or three relations it needs before reading the parts. (Quick jumps between unlinked questions)
- Read the set-up as physics before the numbers: what does each object do to the wave or particle? A wave hitting a plate, wall or closed end reflects, and the reflection meets the incident wave. (Ch 11 (c): a reflector set-up read as diffraction, 6 marks)
- Turn a graph value into a picture before answering: Φ = 0 means the coil's plane lies along B, so its normal is at 90°. Read graph values at gridlines and check them against the axis label (A², not A). (Epoch 1: TMJC Q6 (b)(i), ASRJC Q4 (b)(iii))

- **Graph gradients in under a minute (A-level P2: slow):** draw the line or tangent with a ruler and run it to the edges of the grid. Read two points on gridline crossings, at least half the line apart. Write both coordinates, then Δy/Δx with units. Don't hunt for perfect points.
- **Tangents (Epoch 2: two tangents off, 3 marks with the knock-on):** lay the ruler so it touches the curve at that one point only, with equal gaps opening on both sides. Draw it long, across most of the grid, then read the gradient as above.
- **Sketching a graph derived from a given one (Epoch 2: P–t and Q–t, 3 marks):** go point by point at t = 0, T/4, T/2, 3T/4 and T. Write the value and the gradient of the new quantity at each, then join them. P ∝ I²: never negative, peaks twice per cycle. Q is the area under I–t: it is largest where I crosses zero and back to zero after a full cycle.
- **Never leave a drawing blank (A-level P2: left out or wrong):** mark the start value, the end value or asymptote, the shape (how the gradient changes), and one labelled key point (τ, T/2, a node). Check it against the physics or the graph before it. Right start and right shape is usually 1 mark already.
- **Long numeric chains (A-level P2: one ~40 s entry that can't be checked; corrected by the user, 13 Nov):** algebra first is right, but don't type the result as one line. (1) Before typing, estimate the power of ten by hand: each value to 1 s.f., add and subtract the exponents (about 10 s). (2) Enter it in 2–3 chunks you can judge: numerator, then denominator, or one physical sub-quantity at a time (energy per decay, then number of decays). Keep each in Ans or STO. (3) Accept the answer if its power of ten matches the estimate; if not, re-enter only the chunk that disagrees. With messy powers, type only the mantissas and do the 10ⁿ by hand. Write each chunk down to 3–4 s.f., which also keeps ecf.

**Gravitation (6/6 sets)**
- ΔU = GMm(1/r₁ − 1/r₂), never GMm/Δr: U ∝ −1/r is not linear in r. (Ch 8)
- φ ∝ −1/r, but g ∝ 1/r². g is minus the potential gradient. (Ch 8; again in Epoch 3: g = GM/r written for GM/r²)
- Why φ is negative, 2 marks: φ = 0 at infinity; the force is attractive, so the external force bringing the mass in is opposite to its displacement and does negative work. Give both points. (Epoch 2 YIJC Q3 (a)(ii))
- Gravitational potential at a point: work done **per unit mass** by an external force bringing a small test mass **from infinity to that point**. "To infinity" is the binding-energy direction; don't mix them. (Ch 8; again in Epoch 1 JPJC Q2 (a))
- Satellite losing energy to drag, three links, one mark each: E_total = −GMm/2r, so losing energy means r falls; E_K = GMm/2r, so E_K and speed rise; faster means more work against drag, turned to heat, so it burns up. Write all three, starting from E ∝ −1/r. (Epoch 1 JPJC Q2 (d))

**Electromagnetic induction (6/6)**
In every set: RI P2 Q5, DHS P2 Q7 and P3 Q6, EJC P2 Q6, HCI P3 Q6, NYJC P3 Q4, VJC P2 Q5.
- e.m.f.–time graphs: ε is the **gradient** of the NΦ–t graph. Sketch NΦ–t first, then take its gradient. A coil entering at constant speed: NΦ rises linearly, so ε is constant. Fully inside: NΦ constant, ε = 0. Leaving: same size, opposite sign. (Ch 18 (c)(iii): the NΦ shape was drawn as the ε graph)
- Force on a coil entering a field: only the side inside the field cuts field lines, so F = NBIL with L one side. Fully inside: ε = 0, I = 0, no force. Check Fv = εI. (Ch 18 (c)(iv))
- When a "show that" check fails, the error is in a physics step above it (here, which sides are in the field). Find that step; don't change numbers until they agree. (Ch 18 (c)(iv))
- Magnet falling through a coil: it accelerates, so the exit pulse is **larger and shorter**. Opposite signs because NΦ rises then falls. Equal areas: each is the total change in NΦ. (Ch 18 (d))
- Flux linkage = NΦ, not NΔΦ. Faraday: ε equals the rate of change of NΦ. (Ch 18 (a))
- Rotating coil: Φ = 0 when the plane lies along B (normal at 90°); Φ is maximum when the normal is along B. ε is maximum where Φ is zero, because the gradient is steepest there. (Epoch 1 TMJC Q6 (b)(i))

**Projectiles and charged particles between plates (4/6 and 3/6)**

- Force in an E-field: positive charge **along** the field lines, negative charge **against** them. **Missed twice now** (Epoch 2 YIJC Q6, Epoch 3 ASRJC Q5: an electron in B with E into the page drifts **out** of the page, and its pitch grows because that force accelerates it). To balance a downward magnetic force on an electron, E points **down**. (Epoch 2 YIJC Q6 (b)(i))

**SHM and springs (4/6)**
- a = −ω²x. Units check: s⁻² × m = m s⁻². (Ch 9)
- Why damping stops oscillations: a resistive force (drag or friction) does work against the motion, so energy is lost and the amplitude falls. (Epoch 2 DHS Q7 (c)(iii))
- Insurance only, don't drill (P4-style data analysis, about 3% of oscillation and 4% of nuclear marks in the analysis; user, 14 Nov). Showing exponential decay (Epoch 2 DHS Q7 (c)(iv)): x = x₀e^(−kt) gives ln x = ln x₀ − kt, so plot **ln x against t**: a straight line with gradient −k. Or show a constant ratio between successive values at equal time steps. A high r on the wrong transform (e^(−x)) proves nothing.
- Resonance, 2 marks: the **driving frequency equals the natural frequency** of the system, giving **maximum energy transfer** from the driver and **maximum amplitude**. (Epoch 2 DHS Q7 (d))
- Vertical spring: start from ke = mg, so the resultant at displacement x is kx. (Ch 9)

**First-law cycles and ideal gases (4/6)**
- Monatomic ideal gas: ΔU = (3/2)nRΔT = (3/2)Δ(pV). Needed for any first-law table. Work done **by** the gas is minus the work done on it. (Ch 13)
- Internal energy: sum of the random microscopic **kinetic and potential** energies of all the particles. Depends on T alone **only for an ideal gas**. (Ch 13)
- Temperature vs internal energy: T measures the **mean** random KE per particle; U is the **total** KE + PE over all particles, so it also depends on the number of particles. (Epoch 1 ASRJC Q3 (c))
- Isothermal ideal gas "in terms of molecular energies": no intermolecular forces, so U is all KE; constant T, so constant mean KE, so ΔU = 0. (Ch 13)
- ½m⟨c²⟩ = (3/2)kT is the mean KE of **one** molecule. Total KE = (3/2)pV = (3/2)nRT. Mass of one atom = molar mass / N_A. (Ch 12)

**Nuclear: binding energy and decay (4/6 each)**
In every set: RI P2 Q7, DHS P2 Q6, EJC P2 Q7 and P3 Q7, HCI P2 Q8 and P3 Q8, NYJC P2 Q5, VJC P3 Q7.
- Energy from a mass difference, one route only: Δm in u × 1.66 × 10⁻²⁷ → kg; E = Δmc² in J; ÷ 1.60 × 10⁻¹⁹ → eV; ÷ 10⁶ → MeV. The data booklet has no 931.5 MeV per u, so don't rely on it. (Ch 20 (e))
- Two bodies flying apart from rest (fusion products, α decay, recoil): equal and opposite momenta; E_K = p²/2m, so E_K ∝ 1/m and the lighter particle takes the larger share. For n + He-4: 4 : 1, so the neutron gets ⅘ of 17.6 = 14.1 MeV. Same logic as an explosion. (Ch 20 (e)(iv))
- α-decay with a γ photon (Epoch 2 JPJC Q9 (c)): name it, "gamma photon". Not all the energy goes to kinetic energy. The photon carries momentum in a random direction, so the α and the daughter don't fly apart exactly opposite; the total momentum is still conserved.
- Why fission releases energy, 2 marks: the products have higher BE per nucleon, so their **total** BE exceeds the uranium's. More energy is released forming the products from free nucleons than is needed to separate the uranium into nucleons. (Epoch 3 HCI Q8 (a))
- Energy released from binding energies: Σ BE(products) − Σ BE(reactants), each BE = BE per nucleon × A. Free neutrons, protons and electrons have **zero** binding energy, so leave them out. (Epoch 1 NJC Q7 (b), 2 marks plus the carry into (c))

**Photoelectric effect (3/6)**
- Threshold: one photon gives all of hf to one electron, so below f₀ nothing is emitted at any intensity. The wave model ties energy to intensity, so it predicts emission at any frequency eventually. Name both models. (Ch 19)
- **Two evidence chains, never swapped** (Epoch 3 P2 HCI Q7 (a) and P3 CJC Q5 (a), swapped both ways). *Photoelectric effect → light is photons:* threshold frequency; below it no emission at any intensity; one photon to one electron, hf ≥ Φ. *Line spectra → discrete energy levels:* each photon's energy hf = hc/λ equals the **difference between two levels**; only certain photon energies appear, so only certain level values exist. The level-difference line is the bridge from "discrete photons" to "discrete levels".
- Photon momentum p = h/λ. Bouncing off a sail reverses it, so each photon's Δp = 2h/λ; force = rate of change of momentum; by Newton's third law the sail feels an equal and opposite force. (Epoch 3 P2 HCI Q7 (c))
- Line spectra from three levels: an atom in n = 3 drops 3→1 directly, or 3→2 then 2→1, so three lines. Longest λ = smallest gap. Energies add (E₃₁ = E₃₂ + E₂₁); wavelengths don't, 1/λ does. (Epoch 3 P3 CJC Q5 (b)(i))
- Visible light is about 400–700 nm. Shorter is **ultraviolet**, longer is **infrared**; write the word in full. (Epoch 3 P3 CJC Q5 (b)(ii))

**Infinite well (3/6)**
- One chain: L = nλ/2 → p = h/λ = nh/2L → V = 0 inside, so E = p²/2m → E_n = n²h²/8mL². (Ch 19)
- Normalising: total probability = 1. For a flat ψ, B² × width = 1; then P(region) = B² × that width. (Ch 19)

**Capacitors (3/6)**
- Series capacitors carry the **same Q**. V = Q/C, so the **smaller** C takes the **larger** p.d. Start from Q = CV, not resistor rules. (Ch 16)
- After a partial discharge: energy left = (1 − fraction released) × ½CV₀², then ½CV_f² = energy left gives V_f. Two equal capacitors in series share V_f equally (same Q). (Epoch 2 SAJC Q6 (b)(ii))
- Discharge sketches: mark t = τ with the value at 37% of the start; the discharge current is opposite to the charging current. (Ch 16)

**Superposition: stationary waves and two-source interference (5/6)**
Double slit: RI P2 Q2, EJC P2 Q5, HCI P3 Q10. Stationary waves: HCI P2 Q4 (microwave and reflecting sheet), NYJC P2 Q4, NYJC P3 Q6. Two sources: VJC P3 Q3. Principle: EJC P3 Q6.
- Source facing a reflector (metal plate, wall, closed end): incident and reflected waves are coherent, same frequency, opposite directions, so they form a **stationary wave**. Minima are nodes, λ/2 apart. N successive minima span (N − 1) half-wavelengths. (Ch 11 (c))
- Minima not zero: the reflected wave is weaker than the incident wave, so they cannot cancel fully. Nearer the source the incident wave is stronger and the reflected weaker, so the minimum readings rise. (Ch 11 (c)(iii))
- Conditions for observable fringes: coherent (constant phase difference); similar amplitudes; for transverse waves, the **same polarisation**; fringe separation λD/a **large enough to see**. (Ch 11 (d))
- Phase difference Δφ = 2πΔx/λ. (Ch 10)
- Principle of superposition: **when two or more waves meet at a point**, the resultant displacement is the sum of the individual displacements. Displacement, never amplitude. (Epoch 3 P2 EJC Q6 (a))
- Forming a stationary wave, 3 marks: incident and reflected waves of the **same type, frequency, wavelength and speed, about the same amplitude**, travelling in **opposite directions**; the wave reflects **at the end of the tube** (an open end reflects sound too); they **superpose** ("meet" or "interfere" is not accepted).
