## Block 1 · tonight

**1. Photoelectric evidence** [3], any three:
- **Below the threshold frequency no electrons are emitted, at any intensity.** A wave model predicts emission at any frequency if the light is intense enough or shines long enough.
- **Emission is instantaneous**, even in very dim light. A wave model predicts a delay while energy builds up.
- **Maximum KE depends on frequency, not intensity.**
- The explanation: **one photon of energy hf is absorbed by one electron**, so emission needs hf ≥ Φ, and hf = Φ + E_K,max.

**2. Line spectra** [2]:
- **Each emitted photon's energy equals the difference between two energy levels** of the atom (hf = E_upper − E_lower).
- **Only certain photon energies (wavelengths) appear**, so the energy levels can only have **discrete** values.

Don't mention threshold frequency here; that belongs to the photoelectric effect.

**3. Force on a mirror** [3]:
- **Photons have momentum p = h/λ.**
- On reflection each photon's **momentum reverses, a change of 2h/λ**. Force on the photons = **rate of change of momentum**.
- By **Newton's third law**, the photons exert an equal and opposite force on the mirror.

**4. Three levels** [2]:
- An excited atom in n = 3 can drop **3→1 directly, or 3→2 then 2→1**. That gives three transitions with three different energy gaps, so three wavelengths.
- **The longest wavelength comes from the smallest energy gap** (λ = hc/ΔE).

**5. Visible range** [2]:
- About **400 nm to 700 nm**.
- Shorter wavelengths are **ultraviolet** and longer ones are **infrared**. Write the words in full, not "UV".

**6. Intensity** [2]:
- Higher intensity means **more photons per second**, so **more electrons are emitted per second** and the **photocurrent increases**.
- **Each photon's energy hf is unchanged** and one photon frees one electron, so **E_K,max is unchanged**.

**7. Absorption lines** [2]:
- Gas atoms **absorb only photons whose energy equals a difference between their energy levels**, which excites them.
- They **re-emit in all directions**, so less light of those wavelengths continues in the original direction, giving dark lines.

**8. Superposition** [1]:
- **When two or more waves meet at a point, the resultant displacement is the (vector) sum of the individual displacements.** Say displacement, not amplitude.

**9. Stationary wave in a tube** [3]:
- The sound wave **reflects at the far end of the tube**. An open end reflects sound too.
- The incident and reflected waves have the **same type, frequency, wavelength and speed, about the same amplitude**, and travel in **opposite directions**.
- They **superpose** to form a stationary wave. "Meet" or "interfere" alone doesn't score.

**10. Stationary vs progressive** [3], any three:
- A stationary wave has **no net transfer of energy**. A progressive wave transfers energy.
- In a stationary wave the **amplitude varies with position**, from zero at nodes to maximum at antinodes. In a progressive wave every point has the same amplitude.
- In a stationary wave, **particles in one loop are in phase and adjacent loops are in antiphase**. In a progressive wave the phase changes continuously along the wave.
- A stationary waveform **does not advance**.

**11.** [1] **2A**, at the antinodes.

**12. Phase** [2]:
- (a) Same loop: **in phase**.
- (b) Adjacent loops: **in antiphase** (π rad).

**13. Minima not zero** [2]:
- The **reflected wave has a smaller amplitude than the incident wave**, because energy is lost at reflection and the wave spreads out.
- So at the nodes the **displacements do not cancel completely**.

**14.** [1] **Force per unit charge on a small positive test charge** placed at that point.

**15.** [1] **Out of the page**, opposite to the field, because the electron's charge is negative.

**16. Ball and wall** [2]:
- **Not conserved.**
- The **wall exerts a resultant external force on the ball** during contact, which changes its momentum. The momentum of the ball together with the wall and Earth is conserved.

**17. Why φ is negative** [2]:
- **φ is defined as zero at infinity.**
- Gravity is **attractive**, so the **external force bringing a mass in from infinity does negative work**. φ therefore falls below zero as the mass comes closer.

## Block 2 · morning, main

**18. e.m.f. and p.d.** [2]:
- **e.m.f.:** energy converted **from other forms to electrical energy** per unit charge driven round a complete circuit.
- **p.d.:** energy converted **from electrical energy to other forms** per unit charge passing between two points.

**19. Terminal p.d.** [3]:
- R up → **total resistance up → current I down**.
- **Lost volts Ir decrease.**
- **Terminal p.d. = E − Ir increases.**

**20. Thermistor** [2]:
- As temperature rises, the **number density of charge carriers increases greatly**.
- This **outweighs the extra lattice vibration**, so resistance falls.

**21. Capacitance** [3]:
- (a) **Charge on one plate per unit p.d. between the plates**, C = Q/V. [1]
- (b) The joined plates of neighbouring capacitors (with the wire between them) form an **isolated section whose total charge stays zero**. So when +Q appears on one of its plates, **−Q appears on the other at the same moment**. [1]
- **The same current flows through every part of the series path for the same time**, so **each capacitor carries the same Q**. They all charge together; none charges "first". [1]

**22. Discharge current falls** [2]:
- As charge flows off, **Q falls, so V = Q/C falls**.
- **I = V/R falls**, so the rate of discharge falls with it. This is an exponential decay.

**23. LDR divider** [2]:
- More light → **the LDR's resistance falls**.
- Its **share of the total resistance falls**, so the **p.d. across it (the output) falls**.

**24. r.m.s.** [2]:
- **The steady direct current that dissipates energy at the same average rate**
- **in the same resistor** as the alternating current.

**25. Power–time** [2]:
- P = I²R is **never negative**. It varies between 0 and P₀ at **twice the supply frequency**.
- The **mean of sin² over a cycle is ½**, so mean power = ½P₀.

**26. Gravitational potential** [2]:
- **Work done per unit mass by an external force**
- in bringing a **small test mass from infinity to that point**.

**27.** [1] **Gravitational force per unit mass** on a small test mass at that point.

**28. Geostationary** [3]:
- **Period 24 h**, the same as Earth's rotation.
- It moves **west to east**, the same direction as Earth's rotation.
- Its orbit is **in the equatorial plane** (directly above the equator).

**29. Satellite and drag** [3]:
- Total energy **E = −GMm/2r decreases** (becomes more negative), **so r decreases**.
- **E_K = GMm/2r increases, so the speed increases.**
- The **loss in GPE is twice the gain in KE**. The difference is work done against drag, which becomes heat.

**30. SHM** [2]:
- **Acceleration is directly proportional to the displacement** from a fixed point (the equilibrium position)
- and **always directed towards that point** (a = −ω²x).

**31. Damping** [3]:
- Damping: **amplitude decreases with time because resistive forces do work against the motion and remove energy**.
- **Light:** it oscillates with gradually decreasing amplitude.
- **Critical:** it returns to equilibrium in the **shortest time without oscillating**. **Heavy:** it returns slowly, without oscillating.

**32. Resonance** [3]:
- Resonance: **when the driving frequency equals the natural frequency, the amplitude is maximum**
- because there is **maximum energy transfer** from the driver.
- More damping gives a **lower, broader peak**, with the **peak shifted slightly to a lower frequency**.

**33. Mass defect and binding energy** [2]:
- **Mass defect:** the **total mass of the separate nucleons minus the mass of the nucleus**.
- **Binding energy:** the **energy needed to separate the nucleus completely into free nucleons** (= Δm c²).

**34. Fission** [2]:
- The products (medium-mass nuclei) have a **higher binding energy per nucleon** than the heavy nucleus.
- So their **total binding energy is greater**, and the **difference is released**.

**35. Random and spontaneous** [2]:
- **Random:** you **cannot predict which nucleus will decay, or when**. The evidence is the **fluctuations in count rate**.
- **Spontaneous:** decay is **not affected by external conditions** (temperature, pressure, chemical state). The evidence is that the **rate is unchanged when these conditions change**.

**36. λ and t½** [2]:
- **Decay constant:** the **probability per unit time that a nucleus decays**.
- **Half-life:** the **time for the number of undecayed nuclei (or the activity) to halve**.

**37. α-decay from rest** [3]:
- **The initial momentum is zero**, and momentum is conserved.
- So the α and the daughter have **equal and opposite momenta**, and move in opposite directions.
- **E_K = p²/2m** with the same p, so **E_K ∝ 1/m**, and the lighter α takes most of the kinetic energy.

**38. With a γ photon** [2]:
- **The photon carries momentum** (and energy).
- The total momentum is still zero, so the α and the daughter **only need to balance the photon's momentum together**. Their momenta are no longer exactly opposite.

**39. Electric potential** [2]:
- **Work done per unit positive charge by an external force**
- in bringing a **small positive test charge from infinity to that point**.

**40. Field lines and equipotentials** [2], any two:
- They are **always perpendicular** to each other.
- The field points in the direction of **decreasing potential**.
- **Closer equipotentials mean a stronger field** (E = −dV/dx).

**41. u_min and the plate separation** [2]:
- **The work done by the field across the gap = qEd = q(V/d)d = qV.**
- So ½mu² = qV, and **d cancels**: doubling d halves E but doubles the distance.

**42. Circle in B** [3]:
- **The magnetic force Bqv is always perpendicular to the velocity.**
- It **does no work, so the speed is constant**, and the force has constant magnitude.
- A **constant-magnitude force always perpendicular to the velocity provides the centripetal force**, so the path is a circle with r = mv/Bq.

**43. No work** [2]:
- **The force is perpendicular to the displacement** at every instant.
- **W = Fs cos 90° = 0**, so the KE and the speed are constant.

**44. Velocity selector** [3]:
- **The electric force qE and the magnetic force Bqv act in opposite directions.**
- A particle passes undeflected when **qE = Bqv, so v = E/B**. This is independent of q and m.
- **Faster particles:** Bqv > qE, so they are **deflected in the direction of the magnetic force**. Slower ones go towards the electric force.

**45. Period independent of speed** [2]:
- **r = mv/Bq, so r ∝ v**: a faster particle travels a proportionally larger circle.
- **T = 2πr/v = 2πm/Bq**, so v cancels.

## Block 3 · morning, hedges

**46.** [2]
- **Specific heat capacity:** energy needed **per unit mass per unit temperature rise**.
- **Specific latent heat of fusion:** energy needed **per unit mass to change solid to liquid at constant temperature**.

**47. Melting** [2]:
- The energy supplied **increases the potential energy of the molecules** (it breaks bonds and increases their separation)
- and **not their mean kinetic energy**. Temperature depends on mean KE, so it stays constant.

**48. Internal energy** [2]:
- **The sum of the random kinetic and potential energies of all the molecules.**
- An ideal gas has **no intermolecular forces, so the PE is zero**. U is then all KE, which depends only on T.

**49. First law** [1]:
- **ΔU = Q + W**, where Q is **heat supplied to** the system and W is **work done on** the system.

**50. Systematic vs random errors** [2]:
- **Random errors:** readings scatter **on both sides** of the true value, and averaging repeated readings **reduces** them.
- **Systematic errors:** the readings are off **by the same amount, in the same direction** each time (e.g. a zero error), so **repeating does not reduce** them.

**51. Accuracy vs precision** [2]:
- **Accuracy:** how **close a measurement is to the true value**.
- **Precision:** how **close repeated readings are to each other** (a small spread).

**52. Polarisation** [2]:
- The **oscillations are confined to one plane** (one direction), **perpendicular to the direction of propagation**.
- **Only transverse waves** can be polarised.

**53. Conservation of momentum** [2]:
- **The total momentum of a system is constant if no resultant external force acts on it.**
- In an **elastic collision, total kinetic energy is conserved** (relative speed of approach = relative speed of separation).

**54. Momentum from Newton's laws** [2]:
- By **Newton's third law**, the forces the bodies exert on each other are **equal and opposite**, and they act for the **same time**.
- So the **impulses are equal and opposite**, and the **changes in momentum cancel**. The total momentum is unchanged.
