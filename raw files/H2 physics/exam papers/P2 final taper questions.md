## 1 Quantities and measurement

The speed $v$ of a transverse wave on a stretched string is thought to be given by

$$ v = \sqrt{\frac{T}{\mu}} $$

where $T$ is the tension in the string and $\mu$ is the mass per unit length of the string.

(a) Show that the equation is homogeneous with respect to SI base units. [2]

(b) A student records the following measurements.

- tension $T = (25.0 \pm 0.5)$ N
- mass of string $m = (3.6 \pm 0.1)$ g
- length of string $L = (1.20 \pm 0.01)$ m

Calculate $v$, and its absolute uncertainty. Give your answer to an appropriate number of significant figures. [4]

(c) After the experiment, the student notices that the balance reads 0.2 g with nothing on its pan.

(i) State the type of error this introduces, and explain whether repeating the measurement of $m$ several times and averaging would reduce it. [2]

(ii) Determine the corrected value of $v$. [1]

(iii) State whether the error in (c) affects the precision or the accuracy of $v$. [1]

(d) A ball moving at 8.0 m s⁻¹ due east is deflected so that, 0.50 s later, it moves at 6.0 m s⁻¹ due north.

(i) By drawing a vector diagram, determine the magnitude and direction of the change in velocity of the ball. [3]

(ii) Calculate the magnitude of the average acceleration of the ball. [1]

(iii) Explain why the change in velocity is much larger in magnitude than the change in speed. [1]

(e) Estimate the kinetic energy of an elite sprinter running at top speed. [2]
