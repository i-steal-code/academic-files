## 1. Habits, in order of how much they cost you

1. **Graphs and drawings** (top leak)
   - **Tangent:** the ruler touches the curve at one point only, with equal gaps on both sides. Draw it long.
   - **Gradient:** two points on gridline crossings, at least half the line apart. Write both points, then Δy/Δx with units.
   - **A graph built from another graph:** work out the value and the gradient at 0, T/4, T/2, 3T/4 and T, then join them.
   - **Never leave a sketch blank:** mark the start value, the end value or asymptote, the shape, and one labelled point.
2. **Reading the stem**
   - Underline every defined symbol, every listed observation, and every time-word (after, initially, when).
   - Use a defined symbol exactly as the question defines it.
   - "Change in X" means final − initial, with its sign.
   - Give as many points as the part has marks.
3. **Copying and calculation**
   - Check a formula's units on both sides before you use it. This catches GM/r written for GM/r².
   - Estimate the power of ten by hand first.
   - Enter long working in 2–3 chunks you can check.
4. **Circuit set-up**
   - Write V = IR for each component before stating any direction of change.
   - Every resistor in the loop goes into the total.

## 3. Facts you have lost marks on (don't repeat them)

**New from Epoch 3 Papers 2 and 3 (read these first):**

- **Two evidence chains. Don't swap them** (you swapped them both ways).
  - *Photoelectric effect shows light is photons:* there is a threshold frequency, and below it no electrons are emitted at any intensity. One photon gives its energy to one electron, so emission needs hf ≥ Φ.
  - *Line spectra show discrete energy levels:* each photon's energy equals the **difference between two levels**. Only certain photon energies appear, so only certain levels exist.
- **Photon momentum:** p = h/λ. Reflection reverses it, so Δp = 2h/λ. Force = rate of change of momentum, and by Newton's third law the sail feels an equal, opposite force.
- **Three levels give three lines:** 3→1, or 3→2 then 2→1. The longest λ comes from the smallest gap. Visible light is about 400–700 nm: shorter is **ultraviolet**, longer is **infrared**. Write the word in full.
- **Superposition:** "**when two or more waves meet at a point**, the resultant displacement is the sum of the individual displacements."
- **Forming a stationary wave:**
  - the incident and reflected waves have the same type, frequency, wavelength and speed, and about the same amplitude;
  - they travel in opposite directions;
  - the wave reflects **at the end of the tube** (an open end reflects sound too);
  - the two waves **superpose**.
- **Stationary-wave amplitude** = the amplitude at an antinode = 2A. Sketch both extreme positions, with one of them dotted.
- **Mass to energy:** use the booklet constants (933.75 MeV per u), not 931.5.
- **Ball hitting a wall:** its momentum is not conserved because the wall exerts an external force on it. Name that force.
- **Read the end state:** "remains ice" means only mcΔθ, with no latent heat.

**From earlier sets:**

- **Force on a negative charge points against E.** You've missed this twice. An electron in B with E into the page drifts out of the page, and its helix pitch grows.
- **Minimum speed to cross between plates:** ½mu² = eV, so u = √(2eV/m). The plate spacing cancels out.
- **Electric field strength:** force per unit charge on a small **positive** test charge.
- **Why φ is negative,** 2 points:
  1. φ = 0 at infinity.
  2. Gravity is attractive, so the external force bringing a mass in from infinity does negative work.
- **Fission:** the products have higher BE per nucleon, so their **total** BE is greater than the uranium's, and the difference is released.
- **Energy released** = Σ BE(products) − Σ BE(reactants). Free nucleons and electrons have **zero** BE.
- **α-decay with a γ photon:** the photon carries energy and momentum in a random direction, so the α and the daughter don't recoil exactly opposite.
- **Internal resistance:** more current means a bigger Ir, so the terminal p.d. falls.
- **Damping:** a resistive force does work against the motion, so energy is lost and the amplitude falls.
- **Resonance:** the driving frequency equals the natural frequency, giving maximum energy transfer and maximum amplitude.
- **Uncertainty:**
  - Add percentage uncertainties for × and ÷.
  - Add absolute uncertainties for + and −.
  - A power multiplies the percentage uncertainty.
  - A σ the question defines is used as defined.

## 4. Explanation chains (one link per mark)

- **Satellite losing energy:** E_total = −GMm/2r drops, so r drops → E_K = GMm/2r rises → it moves faster through denser air → more work done against drag → it heats up.
- **Circuit change:** R up → total R up → I down → p.d. across the fixed resistors (IR) down → p.d. across the changed part up.
- **Charged particle in B:** the force is always perpendicular to the velocity → no work done, so speed is constant → constant force magnitude → circular motion. Add a velocity component along B and the path becomes a helix.
- **Velocity selector:** qE = Bqv. Only particles with speed v = E/B pass undeflected. Faster ones are deflected by the magnetic force, slower ones by the electric force.
- **Photoelectric evidence for photons:** one photon is absorbed by one electron, so emission needs hf ≥ Φ. Intensity changes the number of photons, not the energy each one carries.
- **Line spectra and energy levels:** each line is one wavelength, so one photon energy, so a transition between two fixed (discrete) energy levels.
- **Field lines:** they go from + to −, meet each surface at right angles, and their number is proportional to the charge.
- **Collisions:** total momentum is conserved when no resultant external force acts. Kinetic energy is conserved only in an elastic collision. For "can both stop?", check the sign of the total momentum.
- **Stationary wave:** the incident and reflected waves (same frequency, opposite directions) superpose. Nodes are λ/2 apart.
