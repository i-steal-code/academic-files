ch 9 oscillations

they ask if motion is SHM: what must be true of a
a is directly proportional to the displacement from the point of equilibrium and always points in the direction of that point.

given x = x0 sin(wt) or cos: find v, a, max values, phase
v = +- w * sqrt(x^2-x_0^2), a = -wx^2, max v when x = 0 (equilibrium point) so v_max = +- w * x_0, max a at amplitudes so a_max = -wx_0^2
time taken for 1 full oscillation = T, frequency f = 1/T, angular velocity w = 2pi * f.

energy of SHM: where KE max / PE max / total; what damping does
KE max at equilibrium point x=0 and PE max at amplitude point x=x_0, total energy = KE_max = 1/2 * m(v_max)^2. KE at any moment is 1/2 mv^2, PE is found by subtracting KE from total energy. to use these equations, substitute with v equation. damping reduces total energy by making the energy transfer lossy.

light / heavy / critical damping: sketch and what happens to amplitude
light damping still has oscillation, but at lower amplitude as time goes on. critical damping only has 1 amplitude; object reaches point of equilibrium once and just stops. in heavy damping, oscillation is similar to critical damping except that the return to equilibrium is much slower and may often end up not happening. 

forced oscillation / resonance: what peaks, what driving frequency does
forced oscillation compensates for energy losses that cause damping by driving the oscillation itself. everything has a natural frequency where it resonates strongly with that particular frequency of oscillation. 

ch 10 wave motion

progressive wave: what travels, what the particles do
a progressive wave drives oscillation of particles in a wave-like motion. each particle oscillates at a slightly different phase to their adjacent particles, forming the wave shape of a progressive wave.

transverse vs longitudinal: oscillation direction vs travel direction
transverse waves have particles that oscillate perpendicular to direction of wave propogation, while longitudinal waves oscillate particles in the same direction as wave propogation. 

read a displacement-distance snapshot: find lambda, phase, direction of travel
v = f * lambda, 1 period of wave is wavelength, phase difference is 2pi/lambda * dx 

read a displacement-time graph at one point: find T, f, phase
phase difference = 2pi/f * dt, period if one wavelength, f = 1/T

intensity / power / amplitude: which proportions; inverse square setup
intensity = 4pi*A/r^2, intensity inversely proportional to square of radius and directly proportional to amplitude 
intensity halves during first polarisation and has coefficient of sin(theta) for subsequent polarisations

stationary / standing wave formation: conditions on the two waves
standing waves form when 2 coherent similar waves are propogated against each other in opposite directions.

nodes and antinodes: displacement and phase rules along the string/air
nodes are points where both waves intersect at equilibrium, and antinodes are points where both waves reach their amplitude. 

ch 11 superposition

two waves meet: what you add (principle)
principle of superposition tells us that you add 2 waves together to get the resultant wave

interference: constructive vs destructive path difference in terms of lambda
when 2 waves are in phase, constructive interference. when 2 waves are completely out of phase, destructive interference. 

Young double slit / two coherent sources: fringe spacing framework
sin(theta) = (nlambda)/a where a is apeture and n is number of orders. applies for all slit cases.

missing orders or single-slit envelope on a double-slit pattern
missing orders happen when some orders cannot be shown within theta <= 90 deg

diffraction grating: maxima condition; find theta, lambda, or N
sin(theta) = nlambda/b where b is slit separation between each grating. 

single-slit diffraction: minima condition; width of central maximum
width of central maximum is same as the width of 2 

Rayleigh criterion: when two sources are just resolved
two sources are just resolved when the first order minima of one source is aligned with the first order central maxima of the other source. this creates a tiny gap where there is a fade in intensity. 

polarisation: what kind of wave; Malus if intensities asked
intensity halves during first polarisation and has coefficient of sin(theta) for subsequent polarisations

sound / longitudinal molecule diagram: find compression, rarefaction, lambda
compression happens at amplitudes (antinodes) where particles are denser in the region of compression. rarefraction happens at nodes where all particles have moved farther away from the point of rarefraction. nodes and antinodes do not move. lambda is the point between one node to another

microwaves / string / air column experiment: measure lambda from nodes
in air columns, there is an end-correction value to be added to the length of the column to correctly obtain the effective length of the wave. first fundamental frequency (1st harmonic) takes up the full length with half a wavelength. 2nd harmonic takes 1 full wavelength, and every subsequent harmonic adds half a wavelength. total length remains unchanged, so lambda=(n/2)L where L is effective length (accounting for end-correction). ends of the effective length are all nodes.
