ch 9 oscillations (highlights)

SHM test: a proportional to minus displacement from equilibrium
SHM is motion of body where the acceleration of the body is directly proportional to its displacement from equilibrium and is always pointing towards the point of equilibrium.

from x = x0 sin(wt) or cos: name vmax, amax, and the v(x) formula
w = 2pi * f = 2pi/T
v = +-w * sqrt(x_0^2-x^2), a = -w^2x
vmax at x = 0 point of equilibrium, vmax = +-wx_0
amax at x = x_0 point of amplitude, amax = -w^2x_0^2

energy: where KE max / PE max; what damping does to total E and amplitude
max KE at max v, at point of equilibrium
max PE at max a, at point of amplitude
damping reduces amplitude and reduces energy of the oscillation

light vs critical vs heavy damping: one sketch idea each
light damping is the gradual reduction of amplitude until it reaches 0 in > 1/2 wavelength
critical damping is a fast acting reduction of amplitude until 0 in ~ 1/4 wavelength

resonance: what is driven at what frequency; what peaks
resonance occurs when driving frequency is equal or close to the natural frequency of an object, multiplying the peak amplitude.

ch 10 wave motion (highlights)

progressive wave: what travels; what particles do
energy is propogated, particles oscillate about their equilibrium point.

transverse vs longitudinal
transverse is particles oscillating about the plane perpendicular to energy transfer and longitudinal is particles oscillating parallel to energy transfer

snapshot y-x: find lambda; phase difference from dx; travel direction from later motion
lambda is wavelength, 1 full wave. phase difference is either 2pi/lambda * change in distance (dx) or 2pi/T * change in time (dt)

graph y-t at one point: find T and f
T is period, time taken for 1 full oscillation (1 full wave). f = 1/T

intensity: I vs amplitude; point source inverse square (name the idea)
I = p/(4pi r^2), I directly proportional to A^2 and inversely proportional to r^2 (inverse square law for point source)

standing wave: two-wave conditions; node vs antinode (displacement and phase)
2 coherent waves of similar amplitude, wavelength, phase and intensity are passed in opposite directions of each other to form a standing wave/stationary wave. at points where both waves intersect (equilibrium points), it is a node and at points with max amplitude (max displacement) is antinode 

ch 11 superposition (detailed circumstances)

principle: what physical quantity you add at a point
principle of superposition states that resultant wave is sum of waves

constructive vs destructive: phase difference and path difference in terms of lambda
path difference is difference in distance from both point sources, and expressed in terms to lambda to check for constructive or destructive interference. 

two coherent sources / YDSE: fringe spacing framework (small angle)
by small angle approxmimation, sin(theta) = theta. sin(theta) = nlambda/a. so theta = nlambda/a for small theta

YDSE: bright and dark conditions (path difference)
if path difference can be expressed as nlambda, constructive interference (bright fringe). if path difference is more like nlambda/2, then destructive interference. 

missing order: what two conditions coincide (double-slit vs single-slit)
when what should have been a constructive interference on a doubleslit meets what is a destructive interference on single-slit, cancels out and turns into destructive interference. equate single-slit destructive interference fringes to double-slit contructive interference to find 'missing' orders

diffraction grating: maxima condition; what d is; how to find theta, lambda, or N
diffraction grating is d sin(theta) = nlambda for constructive interference. N is number of gratings, so spacing between gratings (d) is length of gratings/N. maxima occur at centre, just like with every other grating.

single-slit diffraction: minima condition; what b is; width of central maximum
b is width of apeture. minima condition is sin(theta) = nlambda/b. width of central maximum is found by taking distance from centre to first order minima and using some basic math and diagrams to figure out the rest. 

do not use the same symbol/meaning for a, b, d across slit types — name which geometry
a is distance between double slit, d is distance between each diffraction grating, and b is width of single-slit apeture.

Rayleigh: just resolved means what alignment of patterns
just resolved means one point source's first order minima meets the other source's central maxima

polarisation: which waves; unpolarised through one polariser; Malus for a second
polarisation only works for transverse waves, and first polariser reduces intensity by half under any condition. subsequent polarisation applies cos^2(theta) coefficient to light intensity

longitudinal molecule snapshot: how you identify compression and rarefaction (not by calling them nodes)
compression is at a point of highest particle density and rarefaction is at point of lowest density. 

measure lambda from nodes on string / microwaves / air column: open vs closed end rule
with L as length having accounted for end-correction in air columns (+c for closed pipe, +2c for open pipe), 
open pipes have antinodes at the ends of L, closed pipes have node at closed side and antinode at open side, while strings have nodes at both ends of L. 
on strings, fundamental frequency is half wavelength along L
on open pipe, fundamental frequency is half wavelength along L
on closed pipe, fundamental frequency is 3/4 wavelength along L
for every subsequently added harmonic, 1/2 wavelength is added to the diagram. 
then, use L and number of wavelengths along L to calculate wavelength
