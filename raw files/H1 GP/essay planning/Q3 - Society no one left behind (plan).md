what extent: achieveable to a large extent (SV) and not achieveable (OV )
*concede that it is not possible for everyone to not be left behind
so SV -> large extent, not fully; OV -> not possible
in your society - sg features: pragmatic, fast-paced, meritocratic, ruthless (for OV)
parallel e.g. (?) US president george bush's "no child left behind" policy ruined the education system, and paradoxically resulted in every child behind left behind their peers globally -> attempts to push for "no one left behind" only backfires -> not achieveable
SV e.g. - notion of sg as trampoline from then deputy PM tharman at 2015 45th st. gallen symposium. People can bounce back if they work; state supports them to catch up (student and workforce examples for R, SV) Those who cannot work have safety nets that catch them when they fall (silver generation packages, etc. for the elderly who cannot work)

intro - In the bustling global economic trading hub that is singapore, society values results in a pragmatic manner no other country does. The fast-paced, ruthlessly meritocratic society of singapore chases results at a speed that some simply cannot keep up with. However, the government has already thought this part through - uisng smart policies and cultural shifts to mitigate this and help them catch up. I stand, that achieving a society where no one is left behind is, to a large (but not full) extent, highly achieveable for singapore.

OV - The opposition is certainly right to poke holes at the claim that singapore can achieve a society where no one is left behind. The notion that we can ensure everyone stays on par with one another is absolute, and in its absoluteness stands the naice ignorance of the key defining features of singapore that render this a far cry from reality. Singapore is a pragmatic society that is obsessed with results, ruthlessly chasing growrth in a meritocratic system that rewards this. But the inequality between competitors was always bound to happen; humans are all innately built different and are unequal in capability in different fields. This can even be seen in the educational system of singapore; after miticulously assessing and grading 12-year old primary 6 students, they are funnelled into secondary school postings of different academic prowess, and subsequently into tertiary education in 3 different groups of very polarising institutions. at every point of adolescence, students are ranked and assessed by performance - a system that identifies inequality and separates those with different skill levels. It raises the question as to whether this system weeds out the weak and leaves them behind in inferior institutions, making the notion of our society achieving one where "no one is left behind" no longer plausible. The very system that powers our society's economic engine is the system that detects and amplifies inequality, then pushes the top forward while the back of the pack get outrun. When such a mechanism powers our society, the goal of a society where no one is left behind is simply not achieveable

planning for R and SV

R - While it feels right to simply conclude that singpore cannot achieve a society where 'no one is left behind', reality tells otherwise. Of course, it is not possible to realise such a society to a full extent, given how inequality plagues the very engine of singapore. but notwithstanding this, it is possible to realise such a society to a large extent. 
> trampoline e.g.
> education system is helping to patch inequality and help those falling behind to bounce back, alleviating problem
> it is still possible to achieve the goal of a society where no one is left behind to a large extent so long as those falling behind can cooperate with the government to work towards upward mobility
> more than just being able to achieve it to a large extent, the action that society has taken shows how it is also a goal that we work toward achieving. 

SV1 - Outside of students, even those in the labour force who fall have a shot at bouncing back up 
> skills future, work study degrees, real testimonies of workers bouncing back up with work study degrees
> upward mobility mechanisms in sg show that we have existing, effective mechanisms for those 'left behind' 
> bouncing back is achieveable even in the next stage of life, society where no one is left behind can be achieved and is pursued as a goal by gov.

SV2 - even those who fall through the cracks are still caught by safety nets to not be left behind 
> merdeka generation benefits, silver package, etc. gov. support systems for those who are unable to work to bounce on the trampoline
> with the robust schemes and eager gov. the goal of a society where no one is left behind is becoming more like a reality than just a naive dream; to large extent is an achieveable goal

conc. - even though it is not possible to support 100% of those who fall behind, sg is is able and willing to help them bounce back and even is prepared to catch those who are unable to bounce back, and the already existing schemes prove this goal is achiveable to a large extent.
