thesis: It may seem that democracy in the present-day determines its winners through votes won solely by their popularity, rather than merit as an actual politician - but this is not the full picture of reality. The reductive and absolute claim that modern-day democracy is merely a popularity contest is something that I do not agree with to a large extent.

OV1 - present-day democracy determines winners through their sheer popularity, with little to no regard to their political merit
e.g. trump, winning by swaying the masses with loud, charismatic speeches and rallies (much more than competitors), social media influence (controversial posts, opinions the masses liked to hear, garnering plenty of internet presence, at a scale that extends beyond borders->echoes back to US citizens to perceive trump as the popular vote), US elections having the popular vote mechanic that literally favours the more popular candidate, popularity of the elected candidates having no correlation, if not even negative correlation to political merit (drop in education level, GDP, etc.)

vs present where people are screaming and cheering in rallies, treating politicians like celebrities 

R - While it is evident that democracy today rides on popularity of its politicians, it is naive to concede that it is nothing more than a 'popularity contest'. simply reducing democracy today to just a 'popularity contest' and having nothing more is failing to recognise the nuance behind the modern day political scene - that democracy is a lot more than just a popularity contest. There is merit in the claim that is is a popularity contest, but i cannot concede the absolute that democracy is nothing more than a popularity contest.
