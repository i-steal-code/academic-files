# '*the arts* are **nothing more** than a *luxury*' **How far** is this true of **your society?**

how far: merits and limits
your society: sg features, resource scarce and pragmatic society
'nothing more than': absolute argument, argue for arts more than luxury

possible examples: 
OV: arts scene is small, is underfunded (? gov subsidy in recent years)
SV: murals during COVID, heritage museums (subsidised), school teaching art, music, social studies and history as compulsory portions of education 

thesis - In singapore, a fast-paced, pragmatic nation obsessed with efficiency and optimisation, the arts are often regarded as non-essential, as a good that only emerges in times of surplus. The intangible impacts of art leave our clinically efficient society questioning the importance of art, and often classify it as a mere luxury, rather than an essential good. But under the shadows, our society actually keeps arts close to heart - acknowledging that the arts are fundamentally essential to the society in intangible ways that do not merit it as "nothing more than a luxury". I stand that the claim that "the arts are nothing more than a luxury" is not true to a great extent in my society, and it is much more than a mere 'luxury'.

OV - It is important to acknowlege that art is largely seen as a luxury in my society
> sg features: small island nation with little natural resources, pragmatic society
> arts have no concrete value outside of luxury good 
> seen in low arts population; only 1 arts school that many academies have to share (NAFA, UAS) with a small population of arts students compared to the extremely large population of STEM students and jobs
> ratio tells us that arts is a surplus in society, which sees no use for outside of luxury; arts does not contribute to the economical machine of sg
> the pragmatic nature of singapore looks solely at tangible, measureable returns as metric for value, and when the same system evaluates art, it sees art as a luxury good that is nice to have, but not essential for driving the measureable returns society is chasing.

R - The absolute conviction of art as "nothing more than luxury" overstates the claim; arts it not simply reduced to merely a luxury, but is an essential good that is recognised by society
> students growing up together, workers, entire community experiences art together
> shared experience in arts, music, social studies and history classes in school, in community clubs, exhibits in hdb void decks (gov. subsidied community centre lessons, NE education so every student has knowledge of sg society and its past, compulsory arts and music lessons in school)
> as long as the state and its institutions maintain that arts are more than a surplus pleasure with continued efforts to push for its ubiquity, the absolute argument that art is nothing more than luxury does not stand.

SV1 - The arts are more than a luxury in sg as it functions as a tool for public discourse
> art of charlie chan hock chye acts as gov. critique
> COVID-19 mural art shares public sentiment
> arts as civic speech to gov. and wider public to share common sentiment so stakeholders can take action
> COVID-19 efforts for better treatment and appreciation of frontline workers
> gov. critique (highlighting supression of opposition) calls for accountability and more regulatory framework
> the arts give the people a voice that has real impact when these voices are not stifled; arts more than a luxury

SV2 - arts more than luxury, has function as it allows us to hold memory of the past, understanding who we are
>sg feature: melting pot of cultures
> arts and heritage museums of various sub-cultures preserve identity and narratives of the past (main art museum and smaller peranakan culture museum) 
> we never lose sight of who we are as a society
> society that does not hold on to identity loses understanding of their narrative and ultimately the human experience, narrowing itself 
> arts holds the rich tavestry of the singaporean identity so we never become a soulless economic machine. treating arts as a surplus thins the human experience into mere workers and consumers; therefore arts not surplus in sg society, is more than a luxury.

conc. - arts is not surplus, it is essential and much more than just a luxury. therefore, not true to a great extent to that arts is nothing more than a luxury.
