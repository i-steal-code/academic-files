# Pillar: Environment and cooperation

**Engine:** Whether countries cooperate depends less on how serious a problem is than on whether the conditions for a binding agreement exist: few players to coordinate, cheap compliance, someone paying poorer countries to take part, and no major player able to walk away without cost. The Montreal Protocol had all four, so the ozone layer is healing. The Paris Agreement has none, so climate action depends on voluntary pledges that change with each government. When energy becomes scarce, as it did after Russia invaded Ukraine and again when Iran closed the Strait of Hormuz, governments put secure supply ahead of climate targets.

**Use for:** environment, climate, sustainable development, international cooperation, international organisations, "doomed", "political or scientific", self-interest, small states, who pays, science and progress.

**Anchor:** A7 (Montreal and Paris) in `02`. The global minimum tax (`04` §H) is the same coordination problem outside the environment, and A4 (Hormuz) shows energy security overriding climate. Singapore's environmental policy is in `09` §D.

## A. Plot

In the 1970s scientists showed that chlorofluorocarbons (CFCs), used in fridges and aerosols, were destroying the ozone layer that screens out ultraviolet radiation. The Montreal Protocol (1987) committed countries to phase them out. It worked because the problem was narrow: a small number of chemical firms made the substances, substitutes already existed, and from 1991 a Multilateral Fund paid developing countries' costs of switching. It became the first UN treaty ratified by every country, and nearly 99% of controlled ozone-depleting substances have been phased out. The 2023 UN scientific assessment projects that the ozone layer will return to 1980 levels around 2040 over most of the world, 2045 over the Arctic and 2066 over Antarctica. The Kigali Amendment (2016) extended the treaty to HFCs, replacement chemicals that do not harm ozone but warm the climate.

Climate change is the opposite kind of problem. Fossil fuels run every economy, every country emits, and the costs of cutting emissions fall on today's voters while the benefits arrive decades later and are shared by everyone. After the binding targets of the Kyoto Protocol (1997) failed to hold the largest emitters, the Paris Agreement (2015) chose breadth over force: nearly every country joined, each setting its own target (a "nationally determined contribution") to hold warming well below 2°C and pursue 1.5°C, with no penalty for missing it. The US left in 2020, rejoined in 2021, left again with effect from January 2026, and in February 2026 gave notice that it will leave the UN climate convention itself in February 2027.

**Mechanism.** A cooperative agreement holds when each member gains more from staying than from leaving. That is easiest when compliance is cheap, the players are few enough to monitor one another, and the poorer members are paid to join. Climate fails all three tests, so its progress comes less from the treaty than from clean energy becoming cheaper than the fossil fuel it replaces.

## B. Eight facts

1. **Montreal Protocol (1987):** the first UN treaty ratified by every country; nearly 99% of controlled ozone-depleting substances phased out.
2. **Multilateral Fund (operating from 1991):** paid developing countries' costs of switching to substitutes.
3. **Ozone recovery (2023 UN assessment):** back to 1980 levels around 2040 over most of the world, 2066 over Antarctica.
4. **Kigali Amendment (2016):** brought HFCs into the treaty, a phase-down expected to avoid a few tenths of a degree of warming by 2100.
5. **Paris Agreement (2015):** keep warming well below 2°C and pursue 1.5°C; each country sets its own target; no penalty for missing it.
6. **US exits:** left Paris in 2020, rejoined in 2021, left again from January 2026, and will leave the UN climate convention in February 2027.
7. **Climate finance (COP29, Baku, November 2024):** developed countries agreed to provide at least $300 billion a year by 2035 for developing countries, far below what those countries had asked for.
8. **Cost of clean power:** the cost of electricity from large solar farms fell by about 90% between 2010 and 2023 (IRENA), which is why many countries keep cutting emissions without the US.

## C. The four conditions for cooperation

| Condition | Montreal Protocol | Paris Agreement | Global minimum tax (`04` §H) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Few players to coordinate | A small number of chemical firms made CFCs, so compliance could be checked at the source. | Every economy and household emits, so no one can check compliance at a few sources. | About 140 countries joined, but a few large economies decide whether it holds. |
| Compliance is cheap | Substitutes already existed, so firms lost little by switching. | Cutting emissions means changing how every economy produces energy, which is costly in the short run. | Countries gave up the ability to undercut one another on tax rates, which costs low-tax states revenue they used to attract firms. |
| Poorer members are paid to join | The Multilateral Fund paid developing countries' switching costs. | Promised finance has repeatedly fallen short of what poorer countries say they need. | No payment was offered; low-tax states moved competition to incentives instead. |
| No major player can leave cheaply | Leaving would have meant losing access to the substitutes and the markets that used them. | The largest historical emitter has left twice, because leaving costs it little in the short run. | The largest economy secured an exemption for its own firms in 2025. |

**Principle.** An agreement is as strong as its weakest condition. Paris shows that breadth without enforcement keeps everyone in the room but lets each member move at the speed of its own politics.

## F. Ending (Insight, Qualifier, Implication)

> Environmental agreements succeed when compliance is cheap and enforced **because** each member then gains more from staying than from leaving, **so long as** the largest players cannot walk away at little cost; **therefore** climate action now advances mainly through cheaper clean energy rather than through the treaty, and is slowest exactly where the largest emitters' domestic politics turn against it.

## G. Exam mistakes

- Writing that the Paris Agreement has failed. Emissions growth has slowed in many countries, and clean-energy costs have fallen sharply; the agreement is weak, not dead.
- Treating the ozone layer as fully healed. Recovery is projected for 2040–2066.
- Using Montreal as proof that climate can be solved the same way without naming the conditions it had and climate lacks.
- Blaming one country alone. The US exits matter, but the structure of the problem (every economy emits, costs now and benefits later) is the deeper cause.
- Ending on "the planet will be destroyed". Land on what is reversible, for whom, and on what timescale.
