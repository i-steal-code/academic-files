## Openers (fill the blanks)

1. **Crisis exposes:** “[Event] did not create [problem]; it removed the option of ignoring it.”
2. **Unequal cost:** “Everyone faced the same shock; not everyone paid the same cost. [A] was inconvenienced; [B] faced catastrophe.”
3. **Trade-off:** “This was not villainy but a choice between [good 1] and [good 2], and each side then came to treat its choice as proof of its own virtue.”
4. **Design:** “[System] was built to maximise [metric]; [metric] is not the public good the title is asking about.”
5. **After-effects:** “The emergency ended. The habits it created — [distrust of authority / dependence on screens / anger over prices] — did not.”
6. **Small state:** “[Big actor] decided [X]. Singapore had no vote in that decision, but it paid the price.”

## Two-case combination (only if the title is very broad)

Use COVID to explain where the distrust and economic pressure came from, and US 2024 to show what voters did with them. Do not retell 2020 and then also retell the election.

**Media:** The infodemic taught many people to treat official statements as one faction’s opinion rather than neutral fact; by 2024 that attitude defined the American election, which was also being argued over two foreign wars.

**Inequality:** During the pandemic, professionals working from home were protected while dormitory and frontline workers were exposed. In 2024 a similar divide reappeared at the ballot box, as voters asked why the US was funding Ukraine when they could not afford rent.
