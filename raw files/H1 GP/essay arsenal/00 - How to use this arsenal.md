# How to use this arsenal

A few deeply understood cases beat many shallow facts. Every use of a case must follow `05 - Paper 1 house model.md`. Main cases: US 2024 (`01`), COVID (`02`), Education (`07`), Singapore features (`06`).

**Per paragraph:** TS (keywords) → mechanism → **two examples** → principle from the pair → qualifier → implication → link.

**Terminal so-what** = Insight → Qualifier → Implication.  
Not a longer prediction chain. See `04`.

---

## Universal lenses (map onto SALT)

These are not topics. They are the *principles* you extract after two examples.

1. Most hard cases are trade-offs between two goods, not stories with villains.
2. A crisis usually exposes weaknesses that already existed; it rarely creates them.
3. Costs fall unequally: identify which stakeholders pay and which are spared.
4. Public trust is often the resource in shortest supply, and policies fail without it.
5. The design of a system — its rules and incentives — explains outcomes better than the character of the people in it.
6. A society can mobilise for a short emergency without being able to sustain the effort for years (short run vs long run).
7. Distinguish benefits that happened by accident from outcomes a policy was designed to produce.
8. Small states are shaped by decisions of larger powers that they cannot vote on (national vs global level).
9. A limit is a condition under which a claim stops holding; an exception is one case that does not fit. *How far* questions need limits.
10. GP has no “solutions”: judge a policy against a stated standard of success (a yardstick).

## When to use which case

Timed chooser: `03`. One main case + **one** comparison sentence. Do not retell two cases.

| Flavour | Main case |
|---|---|
| Democracy, polarisation, media, truth, populism, IR, war, state vs capital | US 2024 (`01`, incl. §H East Asia map) |
| Science, health, work, elderly, inequality, digitalisation | COVID |
| Education, meritocracy, youth exam pressure, schooling inequality | Education (`07`) — COVID only as a one-sentence comparison on learning loss |
| Effectiveness / “enough” | COVID, Education, or SG policy — yardstick first |
| Your society | `06` first; a global case only as one sentence on pressure arriving in Singapore from outside |

---

## Singapore fork (or lose “your society”)

After a global pair: one sentence on **which SG constraint** changes the implication — see `06` (resource-scarce island that trades, trampoline compact, cohesion, state capacity).

---

## Memorisation cap

Per case file: **9 facts**, **1 mechanism**, **3 paired-EG sets**. Colour is optional.

---

## Writing standard

Every line in this arsenal must make sense to a reader who has never seen the conversation that produced it: who acted, what they did, who was affected, and why. No coined shorthand, arrow chains or `≠` slogans in place of sentences (full rule: `.cursor/rules/gp-writing-coherence.mdc`). The labels below are the only shorthand the arsenal keeps, and each is defined here.

| Label | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Main case / comparison** | The one case a paragraph is built on, and the second case allowed only one sentence of comparison |
| **Trampoline** (Singapore) | Help designed to restore people’s ability to work, learn and own a home, rather than replace it; contrasted with a **net** for those who can no longer work |
| **Selection gate** (`07`) | The point where a system sorts people into futures — an exam, a university ranking, or in the US the neighbourhood that decides school funding |
| **Pressure cooker / leaking vessel** (`07`) | East Asian systems that sort so hard the competition harms students, versus the US system that demands and funds too little and too unevenly |
| **The rich control the state / the state controls the rich / the state is rich** (`01` §H) | US and Korea; China; Singapore — three ways a government relates to private wealth |
| **Insight → Qualifier → Implication** (`05`) | The terminal sentence: *X drives Y because Z, so long as [condition]; therefore [implication]* |
| **SALT / SIRDEC** (`05`, `06`) | Unpacking checklist (stakeholders, aspects, levels, time) and effectiveness checklist (situation, intent, resources, distribution, effectiveness, coherence) |
