# Terminating the “so what?” chain

Walk the chain until the **next sentence is no longer required by this title**. Not until the universe ends.

---

## 1. Two “so whats”

| Type | Question | Legal? |
|---|---|---|
| **Justification** | Why should the marker care *for this title*? | Yes. This is evaluation. |
| **Prediction** | And then what happens in the world? | One careful, evidenced hop only. |

The simplest legal terminal is justificatory. “This could eventually lead to…” is where slopes start.

**The tail is already named in `05`:**  
Insight → Qualifier → Implication  
*X drives Y **because** Z, **so long as** [condition]; **therefore** [implication].*

Another “so what?” after that is a new paragraph or a slope.

---

## 2. Stop when all three are true

1. **Question landing** — the title’s noun is in the last line (*lives, fault, doomed, value, enough*…). “Echo chambers” is not an answer to “ruining our lives.”
2. **Value landing** — a good the paper already treats as worth protecting: being able to change your mind; childhood; health; public order; shared facts; dignity; a functioning state.
3. **Evidence ceiling** — the next hop would be speculation.

If (1) and (2) are done, stop even if you can imagine a worse world.

---

## 3. The 2+1 rule (one paragraph)

- **Link 1 — mechanism:** how X works.
- **Link 2 — near consequence:** what we already see.
- **Landing:** so this *is* the harm/value in the title.

Illegal fourth link: “and then democracy dies.” Bigger scale = **new paragraph**, new 2+1.

**Slope vs warrant:** a slope treats possibles as necessaries, or skips *because*.  
Weak: “this will lead to…” / “people will inevitably…”  
Strong: “this already shows up as…” / “where this design exists, we see…”

---

## 4. Write the last sentence first

It must contain the title’s key word. Then write only the links that make that sentence true.

| Key word | Legal tail |
|---|---|
| **our lives** | autonomy, attention, relationships, childhood, work, health |
| **fault** | a named institution had a lever and used it |
| **doomed** | trajectory reversible *or* not — do not overshoot |
| **value** of nuance | better judgments, less cruelty, institutions that last |
| **progress** | who can live better, on what timescale |
| **necessary** | a goal you cannot reach by the alternative |
| **enough / effective** | against a stated yardstick (`05` / SIRDEC) |

If the drafted tail lacks the key word, keep the example; change the tail.

---

## 5. Four stop-questions (first yes that also meets §2 = exit)

1. Does this already name the title’s good/harm?
2. Would a fair opponent still say “yes, and?” If no, you have landed.
3. Is the next hop observed, not imagined? If imagined: cut it, or “this is the risk, and [agent] can still interrupt it.”
4. Am I still on this title, or on the adjacent one? Cut the sequel.

---

## 6. One landing per paragraph

**A. Conceptual** — the consequence *is* the title-harm. (*Not having to think past the feed is already a reduced life.*)  
**B. Comparative** — the alternative does this job better/worse.  
**C. Scope** — not a freak case; it is the default design.

Do not stack all three. That restarts the loop in the marker’s head.

---

## 7. Worked contrast (short-form)

**Slope (do not write):** algorithm → dopamine → indoctrination → clash → elites capture the masses → ruined life.

**Legal 2+1:** feeds reward agreement → people inhabit one-sided information worlds we already observe → they lose the everyday capacity to change their mind, which is part of a self-directed life.

Need civic scale? **New** paragraph: same architecture at population scale → 2024 electorates occupying incompatible ‘facts’ → shared self-government becomes theatre.

## 8. If time dies

> This matters for [key word] because it already undermines [one basic good], not because of a hypothetical later collapse.
