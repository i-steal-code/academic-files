## G1 MS — IOP production

### Item 1 · 2024 RI · "walking the climate talk" [2]

**Ticket nouns:** "the carnivore who doesn't fly" AND "the vegan who travels to see family abroad."

Both required. One alone does not illustrate doubt — it only shows one person is hypocritical.

**Map:** The carnivore avoids flying but eats meat; the vegan avoids meat but flies. Both harm the environment in one way while helping in another, so nobody is truly "walking the talk."

**Neighbours to strike:**
- "off the grid / knitting from scratch" → these illustrate extreme sacrifice, not doubt about who is walking the talk. They are the *consequence* of an impossible standard, not an illustration of the doubt.
- "carbon sin / lump of coal" → rhetorical questions, not examples.

**Planted diagnosis:** The planted answer names "off the grid" and "sacrifice." That is true but answers "Why is the sacrifice unacceptable?" (a different question, Q5 of this paper). The stem asks how the author *illustrates his doubt*. The illustrations are the carnivore and the vegan — named actors whose specific contradictions demonstrate that nobody is clean.

### Item 2 · 2025 HCI · "struggles not so unique" [2]

**Ticket nouns:** "a song from 1972 / Rio de Janeiro's rainiest month" AND "a young Norwegian's turbulent life in 2021."

**Map:** A Brazilian song from 1972 perfectly scores a Norwegian film set in 2021 — demonstrating that human struggles transcend time and place.

(a) Music can convey the feelings of a protagonist from another era and society.
(b) Showing that the same issues take centre-stage today.

**Neighbours to strike:**
- "incongruous" → vivid word, but it describes the *seeming* mismatch, not the illustration. The illustration is that despite seeming incongruous, the song works — which is the evidence for "not so unique."
- "The Worst Person in the World" → film title, not an illustration of the claim.

## G2 MS — Summary unmerge

### Item 3 · 2025 HCI · Benefits of music, paragraph 2

**Scoring units in this paragraph:**

1. **Financial support for the displaced:** monetisation of music (benefit concerts, Glastonbury) raises funds/channels millions to those displaced by disasters.
2. **Boosts morale/optimism:** the concert also bolstered morale / comforted the devastated.

Two units. "Raise funds for reconstruction" ≠ "bolstered morale." One is money; the other is emotional support. A student who writes "music helps disaster victims" has glued both into one blob.

**Planted diagnosis:** "Music helps disaster victims" collapses the financial channel (point 2 in the full MS) and the emotional uplift (point 3) into a single idea. The MS awards them separately because you can have a concert that raises money without bolstering morale, or one that lifts spirits without raising funds.

### Item 4 · 2024 RI promo · Benefits of pets, paragraph 4

**Scoring units in this paragraph:**

1. **(F) Social connections / friendships:** pets create opportunities for engaging with other pet owners.
2. **(G) Sense of community / belonging:** fostering a sense of community.
3. **(H) Combating loneliness:** combating feelings of loneliness and isolation.

Three units. F is the cause (interactions), G is the group-level outcome (community), H is the individual-level outcome (less lonely). A student who writes "pets help you socialise" has merged all three.

## G3 MS — NIT + AQ closing

### Item 5 · 2024 RI · NIT support for "sacrifice is unacceptable"

**Ticket noun:** "convenience."

**Map:** People want to reduce plastic usage *without giving up convenience*. This supports the P1 claim because it shows people are only willing to act if they do not have to sacrifice anything significant — matching "unacceptable to most."

**Neighbour:** "bandwagon."

"We ought to consider the impact of our actions carefully instead of jumping on the bandwagon" maps to *think before acting* — that is about thoughtfulness, not about sacrifice being unacceptable. A student who uses "bandwagon" is answering a different NIT: one that would undermine "just act and not overthink it" (which is actually Q9 of this paper).

**Planted diagnosis:** The planted answer cites "bandwagon" and says "it shows sacrifice is needed." But "jumping on the bandwagon" is about *impulsiveness*, not about *the cost of sacrifice*. The because-noun must name what makes sacrifice unacceptable — that is "convenience."

### Item 6 · 2025 HCI · AQ closing for "exaggerated"

**Title word function:** "exaggerated" = overstated, given more credit than deserved. The closing must judge the *degree* to which music's effects are real vs inflated.

**Good closing example:** "Thus, while music undeniably moves listeners in the moment, the lasting social transformation it is credited with remains largely overstated in Singapore, where structural policies — not anthems — drive real change."

This answers *exaggerated* because it judges the gap between the claimed effect and the actual effect.

**Cousin closings that fail:**
- "Music is clearly an important force in society." → answers *Is music good?*
- "Singapore should invest more in the arts." → answers *Should we support music?*
- "Music does have real effects on people." → answers *Does music matter?*

None of these judge the degree of overstatement. They answer neighbouring questions.

## G4 MS — Harder aperture

### Item 7 · 2024 HCI · "social practice varies" contrast pairs [3]

Any 2 of these 4 pairs for 1m each:

| # | European | American | Contrast |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | Creatures of habit / fixed mealtimes | Eat when they want to | Scheduled vs flexible |
| b | Relish sharing food | Eating is about satisfying bodily needs | Communal pleasure vs individual sustenance |
| c | Relish good company | No one can be judged for insisting on healthy food | Social enjoyment vs personal dietary choice |
| d | Labour — shopping, cooking, washing | Food that requires no preparation | Effort invested vs convenience sought |

**Neighbours to strike:**
- "creatures of habit" alone → this is a description of Europeans, not a contrast with Americans. It only becomes a contrast when paired with "eat when they want to."
- "centrality of commensality" → abstract summary, not a concrete example of how habits *differ*.

**Planted diagnosis:** "Europeans enjoy food together while Americans eat alone" merges (b) sharing food and (c) good company into one idea, and fabricates "eat alone" — Americans are not described as eating alone, but as eating to satisfy bodily needs and valuing convenience. The contrast is communal pleasure vs sustenance, not together vs alone.

### Item 8 · 2024 HCI · Heritage food decline, paragraph 3

**Scoring units in this paragraph:**

1. **(f) Travel → open to other food:** the well-to-do attribute their altered palates to vacations / richer experiences cause them to move away from heritage food.
2. **(g) Food as showing off:** the desire for the selfie to make friends envious determines their dinner plan.
3. **(h) Crave novelty:** this desire motivates them to seek new food adventures.
4. **(i) Prefer trendy restaurants:** keen to be in the know, they queue outside Michelin-lauded restaurants.

Four units. The merge trap is collapsing all four into "rich people prefer fancy foreign food." Each scores separately: travel is the cause, showing off is the motive, novelty is the craving, and Michelin restaurants is the specific behaviour.

## G5 MS — Snap + close

### Item 9 · Comedy · IOP Rayleigh · "generosity" [2]

**Correct pick: Span B** — "we gift the comedian the most generous interpretation of their intentions."

**Map:** The generosity is the audience's willingness to believe/construe that the comedian means well / that the comedian's purpose is constructive.

**Why Span A fails:** "the audience accepts offensive humour they would not usually condone" is the *result* of the generosity, not the generosity itself. Accepting offensive humour is what happens *after* you have already gifted the generous interpretation. The stem asks what the generosity IS, not what it produces.

**Planted diagnosis:** A student who picks Span A is naming the consequence. It is like answering "What is the key?" with "The door is open." The door being open is evidence the key worked; it is not the key.

### Item 10 · Comedy · Summary Rayleigh [8 total]

**Correct pick: Span B** — "exposure to sexist jokes can decrease male perceptions of the seriousness of rape."

**Paraphrase:** Humour that discriminates against women can cause men to take/regard sexual crimes more lightly.

**Why Span A fails:** "The real-world impact of such a stance is profound and troubling" is an empty announcement. It says there ARE impacts but names none. A summary point must name the effect. Span A would score 0 in any summary because it contains no paraphrasable content — it is a topic sentence, not a point.

**Planted note:** A weaker student picks Span A because "profound and troubling" sounds important. But importance is not content. The summary question asks for *negative effects*, and Span A does not name one.
