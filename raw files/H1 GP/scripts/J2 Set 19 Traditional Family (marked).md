## Q1 — ‘a relic of a bygone era today’ [2] (vocab inferential)

**MS payload:** (a) used to be an important / deeply respected / sacred belief or system **+** (b) now lost its value / obsolete. Context required. Either half without context = 0.

**From passage (self):** conventional nuclear family as prevalent domestic arrangement and revered cultural paradigm…

**Answer:** The author uses the phrase “relic of a bygone era” to highlight how the orthodox family structure that was once regarded with symbolic value is no longer seen with the same value in the present.

**Marker:** Context “of the past” may be BOD via “once regarded”. Point A missing — “symbolic value” does not capture the “important belief” in *revered*, *prevalent*, and *relic*.

**Why 1/2:** (b) is there. (a) needed *revered* = deeply respected / sacred, not merely “symbolic”. Relic + prevalent also wanted “used to be the dominant/important arrangement”, not just “had some symbolic value”.

## Q2 — language conveying strong disapproval of marriage [3]

**MS:** any 3 of: *prohibitive* (cost so high it rules marriage out); intensifier *immense burden* (crushing **or** lifelong); rhetorical *why even bother?* (not worth considering); metaphor *shackles* (restrictive). DNA: replacing shackles with another metaphor (jail/prison).

**Answer:** immense burden → tiring obligation; rhetorical question → no meaning, outright ignore; shackles → bondage / inescapable / locking both sides.

**Marker:** name the technique (intensifier) — “intensify” was allowed. (b) missing “for the rest of their lives”. (c) and (d) should be right.

**Why 2/3:** three techniques is the right shape. The unused gift was **prohibitive** (clean 1m). Burden was the weakest of the three you did use — “tiring obligation” is negative but not crushing/lifelong.
